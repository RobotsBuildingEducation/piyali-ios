# Native story activities

The story screen now follows the reference's separate Speak, Radio and Conversation presentations instead of a generic form. The source selects the mode; the introduction pins Speak. There is no user-facing mode picker, typed substitute for recording, read-instead bypass or extra recap step. This is a story checkpoint within an unfinished product parity effort.

Radio shows the original character portraits and signal, with the current spoken line visible only while playback is active. Conversation retains earlier dialogue pairs, alternating bubbles, translations, line previews and an inline learner turn. Speak shows the original alternating sentence presentation, reads each new line aloud and offers explicit Record, retry and Next sentence/Finish actions. Automatic playback grants no reward; Record cancels the preview before starting the microphone. Character portraits use the original 40-entry image map, including its nonconsecutive asset bindings. The learner avatar renders the original companion sprite drawing commands; source text, portrait pools and sprite frames are exported without changing the reference.

Reading and stories publish their actions to a shared fixed footer with space for the main menu. Feedback and primary controls stay in the viewport. Footer ownership is scoped to the active screen and learner languages; account changes and late teardown cannot clear another owner's footer. The footer observes subscription access directly and withdraws at the 300 account-XP boundary. Story mutations, recording evaluation, playback and completion also check access. Strict offline fixtures remain separate from production entitlements.

Radio and Conversation support choice, true/false, reply, select-heard-words and word-order checkpoints. Questions require finished playback; partial word selections cannot submit. A checked answer locks after one attempt. Next starts the next part automatically. The final Finish claims 6 XP once and hands off to the next lesson engine, including when some checkpoints were missed. Speak claims 2 XP once per successful line and adds no extra finish reward. Conversation learner turns require recording and Continue before the remaining NPC audio and question; they grant no line XP.

Playback has cancellable ordered queues, separate line previews, explicit failure/retry and deadlines. Old callbacks from cancelled/replaced playback cannot reveal a question. A line preview cannot satisfy a learner turn or finish the main queue. The native transport now uses [original character voices/personality and native receive-only narration](READING_STORY_AUDIO.md), including the learner voice and buffer/drain completion.

Drafts save selected answers, checked results, heard parts, successful speaking lines, claims and completion across launches. A canonical sorted JSON hash scopes the authored lesson content and agenda; ordinary dictionary encoding previously changed that hash between processes and incorrectly restarted saved attempts. Target/support language, lesson and mode are checked before reuse. Mid-segment Conversation speech results are view-local, so a pending role restarts playback after relaunch. Private snapshot synchronization uses last writer wins; independent transaction receipts protect reward claims across devices.

Native speech scoring uses the source evaluator’s thresholds, stopwords and Latin-only language-likelihood rule. This reproduces a significant source defect: exact matches in several non-Latin languages can fail with no XP; see the checkpoint below. Recognition confidence and recording quality are not phonetic pronunciation ratings. The source Stories hook also uses strict local evaluation, but obtains its transcription through Realtime; native Stories now use the original Realtime capture options and completion report, as documented in [Story speech](STORY_SPEECH.md). Reading has a separate model review with six score cards, now ported in its [read-aloud correction](READING.md#read-aloud-review-correction). Physical microphone and speaker behavior remain unverified.

## Radio and Conversation streaming checkpoint

Radio and Conversation now expose complete incoming turn records and allow playback and learner speech while later records arrive. A question appears after playback once its record is usable. Answers and heard state stay in the transient episode until the finalized episode is validated; matching turns and questions promote that state, while changed content discards it. Advancing past the available segments waits for more content or final validation, and no provisional episode can be saved or claim completion XP. Pending drafts are discarded on background, access loss, provider restart or exit. The stream parser bounds segment indices, turn counts and displayed question data; unlike the web it rejects unsafe records and still requires a valid complete native episode before saving.

Source callback snapshots in [the episode stream manifest](reference/story-episode-stream-manifest.json) cover fragmented records, early turns and questions. The isolated Radio journey answered during streaming and completed once for 6 XP (66.1s); the Conversation journey recorded a learner turn and advanced to the next turn (58.9s). A Radio background/restart journey and six preparation tests, including matching and mismatched promotion, passed. The existing seven speech-controller regressions passed. An initial Conversation run using the automatic transcription fixture did not produce feedback and left that fixture recording; explicit start/stop succeeded, so automatic completion during provisional role turns remains unverified. These are fixture-based simulator checks, not a source/native streaming viewport comparison or a physical microphone test.

Live service verification is mixed: the first four-path run streamed and retained matching Radio turns on the configured and Responses providers and a Conversation turn on Responses, but failed configured Conversation after two attempts. A focused rerun also failed both attempts; it received usable partial turns, then reset the draft and returned a fallback episode. This is not a passing configured Conversation stream and remains a live parity gap.

Follow-up diagnostics found that configured Conversation repeatedly returned scalar numeric answers for single-answer questions, although the prompt specifies arrays. Those runs reset the streamed draft and fell back. Native now converts an integral scalar into a one-element answer array only for choice, true/false and reply before both draft display and final validation; multi-select and invalid indices remain rejected. After that change, a focused live rerun passed on its second attempt with a matching first turn. Its first attempt still failed complete episode validation, so generator fallback and live variability remain open; the earlier failed runs are not erased by this result.

## Strict speech evaluation and non-Latin source defect — 2026-11-05

The current web `useSpeechPractice` calls `evaluateAttemptStrict`; its language-likelihood helper counts only Latin-letter words. In the source, exact sample transcriptions in Arabic, Greek, Hindi, Japanese, Russian and Chinese return `pass=false` with `not-target-lang`, even when the numeric score is 100. The source displays failure feedback, awards zero XP, and allows retry rather than advancing. This is a **known usability defect** that blocks ordinary successful speaking-line progression for those examples; matching it does not make non-Latin Story speaking functionally healthy. The native port previously accepted those exact matches as an unapproved exception. It now follows the current source verdict, score and reward, including its negative-confidence handling. A product decision or upstream fix is needed to change both sides consistently. Existing saved rewards were not removed or recomputed.

The pure [source evaluator manifest](reference/story-strict-evaluation-manifest.json) and [exporter](../scripts/export-story-strict-evaluation.mjs) generate 540 public transcript/audio/confidence cases across nine languages. ## Speak stream completion and interruption — 2026-11-05

The current source accepts any consumed Speak sentence when the stream ends, including a single line without a done marker. If the stream throws after a complete line was revealed, it leaves that line available, does not issue batch generation, and resolves the pending worth gate without the normal per-sentence assessment or story-history write. A failure before a complete line falls back to batch generation. On successful completion the source consumes a final unterminated record; on an exception it does not.

Native now follows these Speak outcomes. Source-consumed text remains provisional while the request runs; when the stream settles, the retained line is promoted with its identity so a completed early recording can be graded once. An interrupted result uses its existing base worth and omits assessment and story-history insertion. A pre-reveal failure still takes the batch path. Explicit cancellation and account/language replacement still discard pending work. Native retains the resulting private attempt across relaunch as an iOS extension; the source keeps its revealed result in component memory. Existing 100 KB stream, 64-line and 8 KB-per-line limits remain native boundaries, so this is not an exhaustive claim for oversized or every malformed provider value.

The [source manifest](reference/story-speak-stream-outcomes-manifest.json) and [exporter](../scripts/export-story-speak-stream-outcomes.mjs) execute the original `generateStoryGeminiStream` callback with fake streams and in-memory setters. Eight cases cover one or two records without a done marker, trailing records, errors before and after reveal, and numeric truthy text. The first source comparison failed three assertions for numeric text; the corrected parser and all five `StoryStreamTests` passed. Thirty related Story Core tests passed.

The isolated simulator’s direct provider-boundary tests cover normal completion, post-reveal interruption, pre-reveal batch fallback and cancellation without promotion or XP.
